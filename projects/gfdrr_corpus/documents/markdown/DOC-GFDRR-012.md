# DOC-GFDRR-012: Colombia Urban Heat Resilience: Cool Corridors & Heatwave Early Action Evaluation

- **Grant ID:** `GFDRR-CO-2027-012`
- **Country / Region:** Colombia (`LAC`)
- **GFDRR Priority Area:** Priority 2: Reducing Risk and Mainstreaming DRM
- **GFDRR Thematic Area:** City Resilience - Urban Heat Resilience
- **Crosscutting Pillars:** Gender/Inclusion
- **WB Classification:** `Public`
- **Fiscal Year:** FY2027
- **Grant Disbursed / WB Financing Informed:** $1.35M / $240.0M
- **Targeted Resilience Beneficiaries:** 3,800,000

## Executive Summary

Evaluation of GFDRR City Scan urban heat island diagnostics, reflective cool roofs, shaded green transit corridors, and municipal heatwave triggers in Barranquilla and Cali.

## S1.0 Intra-Urban Land Surface Temperature Disparities (p.1-2)

Landsat-9 thermal imagery and mobile wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT) transects revealed a 6.2°C afternoon heat-island differential between tree-canopied wards and dense low-income corrugated-metal neighbourhoods in Barranquilla and eastern Cali.

## S2.0 Cool Roofs, Green Corridors & Municipal Financing (p.3-5)

Grant GFDRR-CO-2027-012 ($1.35M) financed urban canopy optimization and high-albedo cool-roof pilots across 48 public markets and BRT corridors, informing $240.0M in World Bank Resilient  & Inclusive Cities lending.

## S3.0 M&E Thermal Reduction & Morbidity Indicators (p.6-7)

Instrumented cool-roof retrofits reduced peak indoor temperatures by 3.8°C, while municipal heat-health hydration shelters and early action alerts protect 3,800,000 urban residents (RF-2030-IND-02).

## S4.0 Informal Street Vendors & Caregiver Protections (p.8-9)

Shaded market arcades and potable misting stations were sited using participatory mapping with women street-vendor unions and elderly day-center networks.
