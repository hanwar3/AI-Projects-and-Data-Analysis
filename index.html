01

Who gets federal hazard mitigation money, and for what?

Do FEMA's three mitigation grant streams fund different projects, and what drives the size of an award?

Data
OpenFEMA Hazard Mitigation Assistance Projects v4 (56,410 records, pulled 3 Oct 2026); sample of 7,781 approved physical projects, FY2015–2024
Programs
HMGP (post-disaster), BRIC/PDM (pre-disaster competitive), FMA (flood insurance)
Methods
χ² test with Cramér's V and adjusted residuals; Levene, Welch ANOVA, Kruskal–Wallis, Tukey HSD; OLS on log award with region and year fixed effects, VIF, Breusch–Pagan, Jarque–Bera, HC3 and state-clustered errors
Tools
Python, pandas, SciPy, statsmodels, Matplotlib, Jupyter
Notebook with code Result tables (CSV)
  • $7.83B

    federal share obligated across 7,781 projects. Florida, Texas, Louisiana, California and Puerto Rico together received 53% of it.

  • V = 0.36

    The project mix depends strongly on the program (χ²(10) = 1,970, p < 0.001). FMA is dominated by home elevations and buyouts, HMGP carries most generator and warning projects, and BRIC/PDM leans to safe rooms and other structural work.

  • +96%

    A BRIC/PDM award is about twice the size of an HMGP award for the same project type, region and year (95% CI +67% to +130%; holds with state-clustered errors, p = 0.001).

04

Pakistan floods, 2010 and 2022

How do the two largest floods on record sit on the Indus terrain?

Data
UN OCHA 2010 inundation; UNOSAT / VIIRS 2022 extent (375 m); Copernicus GLO-30 DEM; Natural Earth rivers; HDX boundaries
Methods
Vector rasterisation onto the DEM grid, elevation-weighted inundation layer, ocean masking
Tools
Python, rasterio, GeoPandas, SciPy, Pillow, QGIS
Code

The 2010 flood travelled as a wave down the Indus from Khyber Pakhtunkhwa to Sindh. In 2022 rain fell directly on Sindh and eastern Balochistan and pooled for months between the Kirthar range and the river embankments. Both footprints concentrate on the flat lower Indus plain, the area I worked on for nature-based flood solutions at WWF-Pakistan.

08

Governed M&E grant portfolio RAG platform

Can multilateral grant retrieval enforce World Bank classification tiers, zero PII leakage and cryptographic provenance while improving ranking from M&E reviewer feedback?

Data
Synthetic/public-domain corpus of 22 section-numbered DRM & M&E documents (88 chunks across GFDRR's 4 Priority Areas, 10 Thematic Areas, FCV & Gender pillars, and 4 WB AMS 6.21A tiers); 32 graded benchmark queries; 6 PII trap field notes
Guardrails
Hard pre-retrieval WB classification gate + immutable SQLite audit log; dynamic PII redaction at 3 checkpoints (chunk, snippet, output); entity-calibrated abstention gate; SHA-256 + HMAC-SHA256 + zero-width watermark
Methods
4-way retrieval ablation (Dense SVD, Okapi BM25, Hybrid RRF, Hybrid RRF + Reranker + Metadata Filter); 2×2 error decomposition; 4-fold out-of-fold HITL weight tuning with 2,000-resample paired bootstrap CIs; Cohen's κw
Tools
Python, Pydantic v2, scikit-learn, NumPy, SQLite, FastAPI, Streamlit, Matplotlib, pytest
Tests
23 passing (classification, PII, tamper, ablation, HITL, API)
Code and README Design decisions Result tables (CSV) GFDRR corpus
  • 0.910

    nDCG@5 after M&E reviewer HITL tuning (4-fold out-of-fold across 208 chunk judgments), up from 0.824 on unweighted RRF (+0.087; 95% paired bootstrap CI +0.039 to +0.145, p = 0.001). Recall@5 rises from 0.781 to 0.926.

  • 0% leak

    100% classification compliance across all 4 World Bank AMS 6.21A tiers, 100% PII redaction recall across 3 independent checkpoints, and 1.00 Abstention F1 on unauthorized and out-of-portfolio queries.

  • κw = 0.46

    Quadratic-weighted Cohen's κ between M&E specialist labels and an automated rubric judge. The automated judge recalls 45/51 primary sections but over-credits S1.0 background sections—showing why human-in-the-loop calibration cannot be replaced by uncalibrated LLM/heuristic judges.

Sandboxed multi-agent DRM workflow & staff AI playbook

When does a multi-agent workflow genuinely beat single-pass RAG on country resilience briefs, and how do we enforce least privilege, crash recovery and staff readiness?

Data
Project 08 Governed M&E RAG + 14-country geospatial multi-hazard exposure table (flood pop., seismic MMI VI+ share, extreme heat days >35°C, 100M resilience target share)
Guardrails
Per-agent capability manifests (e.g. Grant M&E Analyst ≤ Official Use Only; no shell.exec or export.external); Path.resolve() workspace jail; prompt-injection scanner; token/cost/step caps; SQLite checkpoints; mandatory HMAC-signed HIC gate
Methods
3-way architecture benchmark across 8 complex multi-country briefs; OpenTelemetry span tracing; deterministic crash-and-resume testing; 3-tier enterprise staff upskilling curriculum + runnable lab
Tools
Python, Pydantic v2, SQLite, OpenTelemetry-style JSON traces, Matplotlib, Jupyter, pytest
Tests
10 passing (capabilities, injection, path escape, timeout, crash resume, HIC, lab notebook)
Code and README Design decisions 3-Tier Staff AI Playbook Hands-on Lab (.ipynb)
  • 97.5%

    5-part M&E rubric coverage across 8 complex country briefs, up from 60.0% with single-pass RAG, by joining governed grant text with read-only geospatial exposure tables and systematic SADDD/FCV safeguard checks.

  • 100%

    unsupported-claim catch rate when the Critic Arbitrator verifies inline chunk tuples and HMAC provenance manifests before assembly—versus 0% catch rate in unarbitrated multi-agent drafting, at +$0.23 per 100 briefs.

  • 0% leak

    across adversarial tier-escalation (Strictly Confidential), prompt-injection override, shell.exec and ../ path-traversal red-team tests, with deterministic SQLite crash recovery resuming mid-run without duplicate upstream calls.
